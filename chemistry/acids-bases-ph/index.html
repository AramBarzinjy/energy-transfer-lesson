Section A · about 5 minutes

Weak acids and Ka

A weak acid only partially dissociates

Class notes

Ethanoic acid is a weak acid. It dissociates like this:

CH3COOH(aq) ⇌ CH3COO−(aq) + H+(aq)

Because it only partially dissociates, the position of equilibrium is way over to the left. Most of the acid stays as whole HA molecules, so we don't know [H+] straight away.

The picture exaggerates. In a real weak acid far fewer than 1 in 4 molecules split.

The acid dissociation constant, Ka

Class notes

Let HA be any weak acid: HA ⇌ H+ + A−. Like any equilibrium it has a Kc expression. Because this one is specifically an acid dissociating, it is called Ka:

Ka = [H+][A−][HA]

Ka shows how much the acid dissociates. A bigger Ka means more dissociation and a stronger acid.

Two shortcuts Handwritten notes

  1. [H+] = [A−], because one H+ forms for every A−. So the top becomes [H+]2.
  2. [HA] ≈ initial [HA], because so little dissociates.
Ka = [H+]2[HA]  →  [H+] = √(Ka × [HA])

pKa is more user-friendly because it mirrors the pH scale. The stronger the acid, the lower the pH and the lower the pKa.

  • You will be given any value of Ka needed in an exam question.
  • Most exam questions say if an acid is weak, but remember: all carboxylic acids are weak acids.

Section B · about 5 minutes

Calculating the pH of a weak acid

The 5-step method

Class notes
  1. Write out the dissociation of the weak acid.
  2. Construct a Ka expression for the acid.
  3. Simplify the Ka expression using [H+]2.
  4. Rearrange the expression to get [H+] on its own.
  5. Substitute the value of [H+] into the pH equation.
[H+]2 = Ka[HA]  →  [H+] = √(Ka[HA])  →  pH = −log10[H+]
From the notesTry it first

Calculate the pH of a solution of HCOOH of concentration 0.195 mol dm−3. The value of Ka for methanoic acid is 1.77 × 10−4.

pH =

Worked solution

Class notes, corrected
  1. 1 · Dissociation. HCOOH(aq) ⇌ HCOO−(aq) + H+(aq)
  2. 2 · Ka expression. Ka = [H+][HCOO−] ÷ [HCOOH]
  3. 3 · Simplify. One H+ forms for every one HCOO−, so [H+] = [HCOO−] and Ka = [H+]2 ÷ [HCOOH]
  4. Substitute. 1.77 × 10−4 = [H+]2 ÷ 0.195
  5. 4 · Rearrange. [H+]2 = 1.77 × 10−4 × 0.195 = 3.45 × 10−5, so [H+] = √(3.45 × 10−5) = 5.87 × 10−3 mol dm−3
  6. 5 · pH. pH = −log10(5.87 × 10−3) = 2.23
Correction to the notesThe notes write [H+] = 3.45 × 10−5 and get pH = 4.46. But 3.45 × 10−5 is [H+]2: the square root was never taken. Sense check: a 0.195 mol dm−3 acid with pH 4.46 would barely be acidic at all. The right answer is pH = 2.23.

Section C · about 5 minutes

The ionisation of water and Kw

From Kc to Kw

Class notes
  1. Water exists in equilibrium: H2O(l) ⇌ H+(aq) + OH−(aq)
  2. Like all equilibria it has a Kc: Kc = [H+][OH−][H2O]
  3. The equilibrium is very far to the left, so [H2O] is effectively constant. Rearrange: Kc[H2O] = [H+][OH−]
  4. Two constants multiplied together make a new constant: Kw = [H+][OH−]
Kw = [H+][OH−] = 1 × 10−14 mol2 dm−6 at 298 K

Kw is called the ionic product of water. The only value you need to recall is the one at 298 K.

Pure water is always neutral

Class notes

In pure water [H+] = [OH−] at all times, so pure water is always neutral. Handwritten So in pure water Kw = [H+]2, which gives:

[H+] = √Kw  →  pH = −log10[H+]

The notes write "Ka[H₂O]" on page 18. It should be Kc[H2O]: water isn't an acid dissociating in this context.

How pH changes with temperature

Class notes
H2O(l) ⇌ H+(aq) + OH−(aq)   ΔH = +57.3 kJ mol−1

The forward reaction is endothermic (bonds are broken). At temperatures above 298 K the equilibrium shifts to the right, in the endothermic direction, to oppose the temperature rise. So [H+] and [OH−] go up, Kw goes up, and pH goes down.

Pick a temperature to see Kw, [H+] and the pH of pure water.
The key sentenceThis does not mean that water becomes acidic at 373 K. It means that at 373 K the neutral point of water is pH 6.14, not pH 7.

Kw values are from the table in the notes. The pH values at 273 K and 323 K are worked out from them. Be ready to use a different Kw if an exam question gives one.

  2. So as temperature rises, the equilibrium shifts to the right to oppose the increase, and [H+] increases. ✔

Section D · about 4 minutes

The pH of strong bases

Strong bases fully dissociate

Class notes

Strong bases are metal hydroxides Handwritten. Their solutions contain OH− ions. When a base reacts with an acid, the H+ from the acid combines with the OH− to form water. It is the OH− ions that let bases act as proton acceptors.

Strong bases fully dissociate to release OH− ions. For NaOH the equilibrium lies entirely to the right:

NaOH(aq) → Na+(aq) + OH−(aq)
Extra help · compare the beakersA weak acid beaker is mostly whole HA molecules. A strong base beaker has none left whole: every MOH gives one OH−. That's why [OH−] simply equals the concentration of the base.
From the notesTry it first

Calculate the pH of a solution of NaOH of concentration 0.195 mol dm−3 (298 K).

pH =

Method 1: using Kw

Class notes
  1. (1) Use [NaOH] to work out [OH−]. NaOH fully dissociates, so [OH−] = 0.195 mol dm−3.
  2. (2) Substitute [OH−] into Kw = [H+][OH−]. 1 × 10−14 = [H+] × 0.195
  3. (3) Rearrange to find [H+]. [H+] = 1 × 10−14 ÷ 0.195 = 5.128 × 10−14 mol dm−3
  4. (4) Substitute into the pH equation. pH = −log10(5.128 × 10−14) = 13.29
[H+] = Kw[OH−]

Question 1[4 marks]

25.0 cm3 of 0.400 mol dm−3 ethanoic acid is diluted with water to make 250 cm3 of solution. Ka = 1.76 × 10−5 mol dm−3. Calculate the pH of the diluted solution.

mol
mol dm⁻³
mol dm⁻³
  1. Moles: n = 0.400 × (25.0 ÷ 1000) = 0.0100 mol
  2. New concentration: c = 0.0100 ÷ (250 ÷ 1000) = 0.0400 mol dm−3. Dilution spreads the same moles through more water.
  3. [H+]: √(1.76 × 10−5 × 0.0400) = √(7.04 × 10−7) = 8.39 × 10−4 mol dm−3
  4. pH = −log(8.39 × 10−4) = 3.08

Wrap-up · about 4 minutes

Equation sheet

  • Every route ends with pH = −log10[H+]. Backwards: [H+] = 10−pH.
  • Weak acid: Ka = [H+][A−] ÷ [HA] = [H+]2 ÷ [HA], so [H+] = √(Ka[HA]).
  • pKa = −log Ka; Ka = 10−pKa. Stronger acid → lower pKa.
  • Water: Kw = [H+][OH−] = 1 × 10−14 mol2 dm−6 at 298 K. Pure water: [H+] = √Kw.
  • Hotter water: endothermic, so Kw rises and pH falls, but it stays neutral.
  • Strong base: [OH−] = [base], then [H+] = Kw ÷ [OH−].
  • Mixing: n = cV, ICE box, leftover ÷ total volume.
